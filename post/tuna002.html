#TUNA002

Eco-Rambling — ANZOL

October 25th, 2026

tell them you oscillate between rapture and terror like every workday — ]][[

Hello! O For Tuna is going pretty well!
Yes, I took a pretty long break previous to October. So did ]][[, actually. Dubious was doing solo work for awhile. After emigrating and settling in, I was extra depressed for awhile (read: above the usual workable limit of depression), so I spent some time playing games, sewing, etc.
Having now returned to the project, there are way more systems in place than when I left. Thanks, Dubious!!! Quests exist and can be completed! Dialogue exists and can be instigated! A game loop is forming out of the mood piece primordial soup.
This is a major benefit of working on a small team instead of solo. Returning from a break into a project that's since seen progress is uplifting and inspires me. It makes it so much easier to hit the ground running!

The two flavors of this current work arc are:
PLAN THE WHOLE PLOT.
PLAN EVERY FISH.

On the subject of plot. I've written some comics here and there, but they have always been off-the-cuff. Like, the world and characters are consistent, but the comic itself is just whatever comes to mind. Then, we have our bigger game that we had been working on prior to emigrating, which I've put quite a bit of time into planning narratively speaking, but still haven't written many lines of dialogue for. So, I'm a little intimidated about powering out O For Tuna's narrative.
I want to put a lot of care into it, I love the characters, and some of the interactions we've envisioned are so natural and lovely. But, it's intimidating regardless to actually put dialogue down in stone. Before we really tackle line-by-line dialogue though (outside of quest-testing and whatnot), we gotta outline the plot from start to finish. Needless to say, it is way easier to outline the start than the finish. There's some scenes I really want to see, but some of the where's and how's are a little fuzzy. Bringing forth diagetic life and soul is easier for me, I think, than making a "STORY". I really want to do our vision justice, and to make something that feels really heartening to be a part of as a player.

Plan every fish. That sounds easy!
It is not easy. I am such a tryhard about everything.
I'm attempting to make a relatively realistic and satisfying selection of fish per biome. If it wasn't apparent in the game itself (which would be sad to hear,) I am attempting to make a little environmental appreciation post to Brazil and it's many ecosystems and endemic floras and faunas. You wouldn't believe how many fish are endemic to like, ONE river. Like, even if that river is a tributary to another river, that fish is like "Nah, I'm a Xingu ride-or-die." Some fish are even endemic to only sections of a single river. The Amazon River is sort of made up of multiple "environs", one right after another.
I know that 99.996% of the players of this game won't care about any of this, but that doesn't mean I'm going to manually un-care about this. I care about what I want to care about. In this case, it's about putting a lot of love and detail into each of these environments. I'm in a fight with the Rio São Francisco right now. Cool river. It has at least one cool fish in it. But it doesn't really fit into my ecosystem schema so far. We'll see how all that comes together.

This "Plan Every Fish" initiative is also paired with "Plan Every Tree and Plant"... just so you know... So far I have a couple cool tree homies and some endemic bromeliads I'm looking forward to modelling with ]][[. We'll see how much of a nightmare it is once we really get down to brass tacks with that. For now, it's fish focus time.
